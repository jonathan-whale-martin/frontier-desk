Anthropic said it will commit $150 million over three years to the Genesis Mission, a federal initiative to speed up scientific and technological discovery through AI. The money will support making its Claude models available to more than 15 participating agencies, including NASA, the National Institutes of Health and the National Science Foundation.

The company said it first announced a partnership with the US Department of Energy and the Genesis Mission last December, and has since brought Claude to scientists at the national laboratories. The new commitment was announced at the Science: A New Golden Age Summit, hosted by the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy in Washington, DC.

Anthropic said that over the next three years it will provide Claude, Claude Code and API credits to several hundred Genesis Mission research projects. It also plans to work with agencies on priorities including fusion energy and quantum computing, and to offer training, onboarding and technical support to scientists on these projects.

The announcement follows several earlier science initiatives from the company. Anthropic said it had launched Claude Science, an AI workbench, opened 10,000 free and discounted Claude seats for academic scientists, expanded its AI for Science credits programme, and started a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard, a specification for AI agents to operate lab instruments safely.
