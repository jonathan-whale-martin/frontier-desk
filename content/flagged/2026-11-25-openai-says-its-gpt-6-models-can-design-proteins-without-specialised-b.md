According to OpenAI, its GPT-6 models can dream up novel protein shapes and specify the chemical building blocks that make them up, all without handing the job to dedicated biology software. The findings appear in a preprint headed "GPT-6 as a Universal Foundation Model for Protein Biology: Beyond Specialized Models".

The company says an internal research version performed as well as, or better than, purpose-built models on numerous protein design challenges. Scientists could also steer outcomes by telling the system where a protein ought to latch on and what form it should take.

Working with Onava, OpenAI put designs to the test in a laboratory. Of 322 small binding proteins (minibinders) evaluated against five targets, 35, or 10.9%, showed binding to their intended target. Specialised tools still supplied sequences and helped pick which candidates went forward, so the pipeline was not entirely general-purpose.

In a further experiment, GPT-6.2 was asked to rewrite the sequences of existing minibinders and antibodies, and many of the reworked versions also attached to the original targets. Separately, binding was observed in 75 of 88 designs for MDM2, 26 of 42 for VEGF-A and 8 of 59 for PD-1.

OpenAI also acknowledged that the RFdiffusion study, a widely used specialised model, reported higher success rates. It argues that such figures hinge heavily on how stringently designs are screened beforehand and on what counts as binding. Predicting an entire protein's structure remains a notable weak spot, the company said. These are company-reported results from a preprint and have not been independently verified.
