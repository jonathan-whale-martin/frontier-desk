Google has opened a new ranking hub on Search Central, which serves as a transparency point for how Search ranks results, according to Search Engine Roundtable. It gathers details on ranking systems, advance notice timelines, status dashboard updates and routes for giving feedback in a single spot.

Google plans to announce upcoming search updates ahead of time, beginning with the December 2026 core update.

Site owners also gain a dedicated feedback and triage area for flagging problems with particular updates. Submissions carry specific hashtags that are assigned within the dashboard. Google says the aim is to help its engineering teams spot and sort out possible systemic issues, not to handle individual site appeals.

Google has additionally published a set of principles describing how ranking results are produced. The wording states that Search is built to arrange information so it is useful and relevant to people looking for answers, and that users are the priority.

Google has also refreshed its Google-Extended documentation for AI training controls. The control now follows a product-agnostic approach, so its description can remain accurate as the technology changes. Google says how it honours the control has not changed: opting out does not remove a website from Search results, and it is not a ranking factor.

Two new Help Center explainers accompany the update. One covers the distinction between training and grounding, and how Google's various controls apply to each. The other sets out how attribution and citations function in AI Search features.

Search Engine Roundtable has also published a separate piece looking more closely at the attribution documentation for AI Overviews and AI Mode.
