Google has published a new document explaining how it selects and displays links in AI Overviews, AI Mode and other Search generative AI features, according to Search Engine Roundtable.

The document, titled "Attribution and factuality in Search generative AI features", covers how sources are selected, how Google checks accuracy before a response is shown, its ongoing evaluation methods, and best practices for site owners.

According to the document, the process starts with the Google Search index. Results relevant to a query are examined, and the model may run related searches, known as "query fan-out", to find pages covering different parts of the question. The model then cites pages that support its response. Links are chosen by balancing relevance and quality signals from Search ranking with a diverse set of helpful sources.

Before a response appears, Google says automated checks apply. Responses may not be shown for sensitive topics, breaking news with limited coverage, or where reliable consensus is lacking. Claims are also checked against web results, data feeds and the Knowledge Graph.

Google lists four best practices for sites wanting to be cited: make sure pages are indexed and eligible for snippets, publish reliable and helpful content, keep facts accurate and up to date, and monitor performance with the Generative AI performance report in Search Console.

Search Engine Roundtable noted that the document states these features are integrated with Google's core ranking and quality systems. Commentators Lily Ray and Marie Haynes also discussed the document on X.
