Nvidia has reportedly told manufacturing partners, including Dell and Super Micro, to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, according to a Wall Street Journal report cited by TechCrunch. The pause has no set end date.

Nvidia is reportedly assessing whether its regional partners are meeting US export requirements. The report links the move to remote access to Nvidia chips. Chinese companies have reportedly been using Nvidia chips remotely from data centres in these countries, a loophole the US Commerce Department reportedly closed earlier this month. TechCrunch said the Commerce Department acted reportedly grudgingly.

The Journal cited the think tank Institute for Progress, which estimates that remote data centres of this kind supply about 24% of China's AI computing power.

TechCrunch's report is based on the Journal's reporting. Neither Nvidia nor the Commerce Department is quoted in the source material provided, so the details of the pause have not been confirmed by the companies involved.
