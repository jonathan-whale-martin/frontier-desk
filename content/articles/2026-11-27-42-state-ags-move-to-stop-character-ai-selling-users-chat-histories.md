A coalition of 42 state attorneys general is trying to stop Character.AI from selling users' private chat histories as the company goes through Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings, according to The Verge.

The attorneys general say the sale would cover chat histories from more than 21 million US accounts, including more than 2.1 million registered to minors. They argue that the sale offers no safeguards for the people whose conversations would be sold. The Verge reported that the AGs issued nearly identical press releases.

The AGs' main concerns are that there is no requirement for a buyer to honour existing privacy policies, no way for users to delete their data, and no limit on what a buyer could do with it, including selling it on again. They also note that the first bidder was only established on 13 November 2026 and has no parent company or investors listed.

Character.AI filed for bankruptcy in October, following legal challenges including one from Disney. Disney bought some of the company's assets and hired its CEO, Karandeep Anand, in an arrangement The Verge described as an "acquihire". A similarly broad group of AGs has also opposed that deal.

The Verge also reported that several states, including New York, Connecticut, California and Colorado, have passed chatbot laws that Character.AI may breach when they take effect on 1 January 2027.

The report is based on the AGs' public statements; the source does not include a response from Character.AI.
